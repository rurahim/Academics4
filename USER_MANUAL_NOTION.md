# About This Platform

## What is Academics4?

Academics4 is a website that helps **students who have been displaced from their universities** (like students affected by the Gaza crisis) connect with **volunteer teachers from around the world**.

Think of it like a matchmaking service, but instead of dating, we match students who need help with specific subjects to volunteers who can teach those subjects.

## The Problem We Solve

Imagine a medical student in Gaza whose university was destroyed. They need help with subjects like Anatomy and Pharmacology to continue their education. Meanwhile, a doctor in Canada wants to volunteer their time to help but doesn't know how to find students who need their expertise.

Academics4 brings them together.

## How It Works (Simple Version)

1. **Students sign up** and tell us what subjects they need help with
2. **Volunteers sign up** and tell us what subjects they can teach
3. **Our system finds the best matches** based on subjects, language, and availability
4. **Admin reviews and approves** the matches
5. **Volunteers and students connect** for tutoring sessions
6. **Everyone tracks progress** through the platform

# The Three Types of Users

## Students

Students are learners who need academic support. On this platform, students can:
- Create a profile listing subjects they need help with
- See which volunteer teachers have been assigned to help them
- View their upcoming and past tutoring sessions
- Track their learning progress

## Volunteers (Teachers)

Volunteers are people who want to donate their time and knowledge. They can:
- Create a profile listing subjects they can teach
- Receive notifications when assigned to help a student
- Accept or decline student assignments
- Record details of each tutoring session
- Track how many hours they've volunteered

## Administrators (Admins)

Admins manage the entire platform. They can:
- See all students and volunteers
- Create matches between students and volunteers
- Monitor teaching progress
- View reports and statistics
- Manage system settings

# Getting Started

**Step 1: Create Account**

- Choose "I am a Student"

- Click Register

**Step 2: Fill Your Profile**

You'll need to provide:

- **Full Name** - Your complete name (Example: Sarah Ahmed)
- **Cause** - Why you need support (Example: Gaza)
- **Location** - Where you currently are (Example: Gaza City, Palestine)
- **Field of Study** - Your academic major (Example: Medicine)
- **Topics Need Help** - Specific subjects you need tutoring in (Example: Anatomy, Physiology, Biochemistry)
- **Languages** - Languages you can communicate in (Example: Arabic, English)

**Step 1: Create Account**
- Go to the registration page

- You'll receive email when assigned to a student
- Log in and review the assignment
- Click Accept or Decline

# How the Matching System Works

## The Big Picture

Our system looks at every possible student-volunteer pair and calculates a "match score" from 0 to 100. The higher the score, the better the match. This helps admins quickly see which volunteers are best suited for each student.

## What Makes a Good Match?

We consider six factors when calculating the match score:

### Factor 1: Cause Alignment (25 points)

**What it means:** Does the volunteer want to help students from this cause?

**How it works:**
- If the volunteer selected "Gaza" as a cause they support, and the student is from Gaza, they get 25 points
- If there's no cause match, they get 0 points and cannot be matched

**Why it matters:** Volunteers choose which causes they want to support. A volunteer who signed up to help Gaza students should be matched with Gaza students.

**Example:**
- Volunteer supports: Gaza, Syria
- Student's cause: Gaza
- Result: Match! +25 points

---

### Factor 2: Field of Study Match (20 points)

**What it means:** Does the volunteer's expertise area match the student's field of study?

**How it works:**
- We compare the volunteer's fields of expertise with the student's field of study
- More overlap means more points

**Scoring:**
- Perfect match (same field): 20 points
- Partial match (related fields): 10-15 points
- No match: 0 points

**Example:**
- Student studies: Medicine
- Volunteer's expertise: Medicine, Biology, Chemistry
- Result: Medicine matches! +20 points

---

### Factor 3: Subject Match (25 points)

**What it means:** Can the volunteer teach what the student needs to learn?

**How it works:**
- We compare the student's "Topics Need Help" with the volunteer's "Subjects Can Teach"
- The system is smart enough to recognize similar words (like "Pharma" and "Pharmacology")

**Scoring:**
- All subjects match: 25 points
- Most subjects match: 15-20 points
- Some subjects match: 5-15 points
- No subjects match: 0 points

**Example:**
- Student needs: Anatomy, Pharmacology
- Volunteer can teach: Anatomy, Pharmacology, Biochemistry
- Result: Both subjects match! +25 points

---

### Factor 4: Language Compatibility (15 points)

**What it means:** Can they communicate effectively?

**How it works:**
- Best case: Same preferred language
- Good case: At least one common language
- Worst case: No common language

**Scoring:**
- Same preferred language: 15 points
- Different preferred but share a common language: 10 points
- No common language: 0 points

**Example:**
- Student's preferred language: Arabic
- Volunteer's preferred language: Arabic
- Result: Perfect language match! +15 points

---

### Factor 5: Availability Match (10 points)

**What it means:** Does the volunteer have enough time for this student?

**How it works:**
- We compare the student's hours needed with the volunteer's hours available
- Volunteer should have at least as many hours as the student needs

**Scoring:**
- Volunteer has enough or more hours: 10 points
- Volunteer has fewer hours: Partial points based on percentage

**Example:**
- Student needs: 10 hours/week
- Volunteer available: 15 hours/week
- Result: Plenty of time! +10 points

---

### Factor 6: Capacity Check (5 points)

**What it means:** Does the volunteer have room for another student?

**How it works:**
- Each volunteer sets a maximum number of students they can handle
- We check how many slots they have available

**Scoring:**
- Many slots available: 5 points
- Few slots available: 2-4 points
- No slots available: 0 points (cannot be matched)

**Example:**
- Volunteer's maximum students: 5
- Currently teaching: 2 students
- Available slots: 3
- Result: Has room! +4 points

---

## Complete Matching Example

Let's walk through a real example:

**Student: Sarah**
- Cause: Gaza
- Field of Study: Medicine
- Needs Help With: Anatomy, Physiology
- Preferred Language: Arabic
- Hours Needed: 10 per week

**Volunteer: Dr. Ahmed**
- Supports Causes: Gaza, Syria
- Fields of Expertise: Medicine, Biology
- Can Teach: Anatomy, Physiology, Biochemistry
- Preferred Language: Arabic
- Hours Available: 15 per week
- Current Students: 2 of 5 maximum

**Score Calculation:**

- **Cause** - Does Dr. Ahmed support Gaza? Yes (he selected Gaza) = +25 points
- **Fields** - Does Medicine match? Yes (he has Medicine expertise) = +20 points
- **Subjects** - Can he teach Anatomy & Physiology? Yes, both! = +25 points
- **Language** - Same preferred language? Yes, both Arabic = +15 points
- **Availability** - Does 15 hours cover 10 hours needed? Yes = +10 points
- **Capacity** - Does he have open slots? Yes, 3 available = +4 points
- **TOTAL = 99/100**

**Conclusion:** This is an excellent match! The system would recommend this pairing to the admin.

## Smart Subject Matching

Our system is intelligent about matching subjects. It understands that:

- "Pharma" and "Pharmacology" are the same subject
- "Neutraceutical" and "Nutraceuticals" are the same subject (spelling variation)
- "Math" and "Mathematics" are the same subject
- "Bio" and "Biology" are the same subject
- "Stats" and "Statistics" are the same subject

This means even if a student writes "Pharma" and a volunteer writes "Pharmacology," the system recognizes these as a match.

# Different Ways to Do the Same Thing

One of the best features of Academics4 is flexibility. There are multiple ways to accomplish most tasks.

## Assigning a Volunteer to a Student

### Way 1: Use AI Suggestions (Recommended)
1. Go to Matches page
2. Click on "AI Suggestions" tab
3. See the system's recommended matches with scores
4. Review the match details
5. Click "Create Match"

**Best when:** You want to use the system's recommendations

### Way 2: From the Student's Page
1. Go to Students page
2. Find the student
3. Click to expand their profile
4. Look at their unassigned topics (marked with red X)
5. Click "Assign" next to the topic
6. Choose a volunteer from the list
7. Confirm

**Best when:** You're looking at a specific student and want to assign help for one topic

### Way 3: From the Unmatched Tab
1. Go to Matches page
2. Click "Unmatched" tab
3. See students who still need volunteers
4. Click "Find Volunteer" for a student
5. System shows available volunteers ranked by compatibility
6. Select one and create the match

**Best when:** You want to focus on students who don't have full coverage yet

### Way 4: Manual Match Creation
1. Go to Matches page
2. Click "Create Manual Match"
3. Select a student from dropdown
4. Select a volunteer from dropdown
5. Choose which subjects to assign
6. Add notes if needed
7. Create the match

**Best when:** You know exactly who should be matched (maybe they requested each other)

# The Complete Journey

## What Happens When a New Student Joins

1. **Student registers** on the website and fills out their profile
2. **Admin gets notified** about the new student
3. **System analyzes** the student's needs and finds suitable volunteers
4. **AI Suggestions appear** in the Matches page for admin to review
5. **Admin creates a match** by approving a suggestion or making a manual match
6. **Admin sends an email** to notify the volunteer
7. **Volunteer logs in** and sees the new student assignment
8. **Volunteer clicks Accept** (or Decline if they can't help)
9. **Match becomes Active** once volunteer accepts
10. **Volunteer conducts sessions** and logs them in the system
11. **Student sees sessions** in their dashboard
12. **Admin monitors progress** through the Matches and Analytics pages

---

## What Happens When a New Volunteer Joins

1. **Volunteer registers** and completes their profile
2. **System now includes them** when calculating matches
3. **AI may generate new suggestions** pairing them with waiting students
4. **Admin assigns students** to the volunteer
5. **Volunteer receives notification email**
6. **Volunteer logs in and accepts** assignments
7. **Teaching begins** - volunteer conducts sessions
8. **Volunteer logs each session** with date, duration, and topics covered
9. **Hours are tracked** automatically in the system

---

## What Happens During a Tutoring Session

1. **Volunteer and student** arrange a time to meet (video call, etc.)
2. **Session happens** - they study together
3. **After the session, volunteer logs it:**
   - Goes to Sessions page
   - Clicks "Log Session"
   - Selects the student
   - Enters date and time
   - Enters duration (how long in minutes)
   - Selects topics covered
   - Adds notes about what was taught
   - Saves

4. **The logged session appears:**
   - In the volunteer's Sessions page
   - In the student's Sessions page
   - In the admin's Match view
   - In the Analytics dashboard

# Understanding Match Statuses

When a match is created, it goes through several stages:

**Auto Suggested**
- What It Means: The system recommended this match
- What Needs to Happen Next: Admin needs to review and approve it

**Pending Email**
- What It Means: Match is approved, ready to notify volunteer
- What Needs to Happen Next: Admin needs to click "Send Email"

**Email Sent**
- What It Means: Volunteer has been emailed about the assignment
- What Needs to Happen Next: Volunteer needs to log in and respond

**Accepted**
- What It Means: Volunteer said yes to the assignment
- What Needs to Happen Next: Admin can activate the match

**Active**
- What It Means: Teaching is in progress
- What Needs to Happen Next: Volunteer can log sessions

**Paused**
- What It Means: Temporarily on hold
- What Needs to Happen Next: Admin can resume when ready

**Completed**
- What It Means: Teaching relationship has ended
- What Needs to Happen Next: Match is archived

**Rejected**
- What It Means: Volunteer declined the assignment
- What Needs to Happen Next: Admin needs to find another volunteer

**Not Compatible**
- What It Means: Match was determined to not work
- What Needs to Happen Next: Admin should try different pairing

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Questions from Students

**How long until I get a teacher?**
Usually within a few days. It depends on finding a volunteer who can teach your specific subjects and speaks your language.

**Can I have more than one teacher?**
Yes! If you need help with different subjects, you might have different volunteers for each.

**What if my teacher isn't helping me well?**
Tell the admin. They can pause the match and find you a different volunteer.

**How do I add more subjects I need help with?**
Go to your Profile page and add new topics to "Topics Need Support." The admin will see these and find volunteers for them.

---

## Questions from Volunteers

**How many students will I get?**
You control this by setting your "Max Students" in your profile. You won't be assigned more than this number.

**Can I say no to an assignment?**
Yes. When you receive an assignment, you can click "Accept" or "Decline." No pressure.

**How do I record my tutoring sessions?**
Go to Sessions > Log Session > Fill in the details > Save. Do this after each tutoring session.

**What if I need to take a break?**
Contact the admin. They can pause your matches. Or reduce your available hours in your profile.

**Do I have to teach all subjects I listed?**
No. You list all subjects you CAN teach. Admin will only assign you for subjects that match student needs.

---

## Questions from Admins

**How do I find the best volunteer for a student?**
Check the AI Suggestions tab. The system already ranked volunteers by compatibility score.

**Can I override what the system suggests?**
Absolutely. AI suggestions are just recommendations. You can create any match manually.

**How do I stop a volunteer from getting more students?**
Use the "Lock" feature on their profile. This prevents new assignments while keeping existing ones.

**How do I see all activity?**
The Analytics page shows all statistics. The Dashboard gives a quick overview.

**Can I export the data?**
Yes. Go to Analytics and use the Export buttons for Excel or PDF reports.

## Understanding the Colors

- **Green** - Good (Active, Completed, Matched)
- **Yellow/Orange** - Attention needed (Pending, Waiting)
- **Blue** - In progress (Email sent, Processing)
- **Red** - Problem (Rejected, Unassigned, Error)
- **Gray** - Inactive (Paused, Disabled)

## Capacity Explained

When you see "Capacity: 2/5" for a volunteer, it means:
- **2** = Number of students currently assigned
- **5** = Maximum students they said they can handle
- **3** = Available slots (5 minus 2)

## Coverage Explained

When you see "67% covered" for a student, it means:
- The student listed some topics they need help with
- 67% of those topics have been assigned to volunteers
- 33% still need volunteers
